How to add a watermark to a PDF

A watermark is a word written in large, pale letters across the page. It tells every reader what the document is (a draft, a confidential copy) and, on a copy of your passport, what it may be used for.

Step by step

  1. Open the editor and open your PDF. It is not uploaded.
  2. Click Tools, then Watermark.
  3. Type the text (up to 60 characters), choose its strength and colour, then click Add watermark.
  4. Check a page, then save. Chrome and Edge can save into the file itself; to keep the original without a watermark, use Download copy instead.

The options

  • Text: one line. The editor sizes it to run corner to corner on every page, whatever the page size.
  • Strength: Light, Medium or Strong. The letters are transparent, so the text below stays readable. Light suits text documents; Strong is better on photos and scans, where a pale watermark disappears in the picture.
  • Colour: red, grey or blue.

Watermarking copies of your ID documents

Landlords, employers and some online services ask for a copy of an identity card, a passport or a payslip. A copy that circulates can be reused by someone else, for example to open an account in your name. Many data-protection authorities, including the French CNIL, recommend watermarking every copy you send.

A good watermark for that says who it is for, why, and when. For example:

  • Copy for Smith Lettings – flat application – Nov 2026
  • For HR at Acme only – contract 2026

A copy marked this way is much harder to reuse for anything else. Use a strength that keeps your name and the document number readable: the recipient still needs to check them.

Other good uses

  • DRAFT on a document still under review, so nobody signs or forwards the wrong version.
  • CONFIDENTIAL or INTERNAL on documents that must not leave a team.
  • COPY or SPECIMEN on examples, so they are not mistaken for the real thing.
  • A recipient's name on each copy of a document you share with several people: if one copy leaks, you know which.

What a watermark cannot do

The watermark is written into the page itself, not added as a removable comment, so it survives printing and copying. But it is a label, not a lock. Someone with time and a PDF editor can remove it, or crop it out of a scan. If a document must stay secret, protect it with a password; if some details must not be seen at all, redact them before you add the watermark.

If you change your mind

Undo (⌘Z on a Mac, Ctrl+Z elsewhere) removes the watermark from every page at once, as long as the PDF is still open. After saving, open the original again, or use Download copy from the start to keep both versions.
