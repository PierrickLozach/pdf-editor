How to edit a Word or LibreOffice document in your browser

On a borrowed computer, a Chromebook, or a work laptop where you cannot install anything, you may still need to fix a Word document. The editor opens it in your browser, on real pages, and the file never leaves your device.

Which files it opens

  • Word documents (.docx) and Word templates (.dotx).
  • LibreOffice and OpenOffice documents (.odt) and templates (.ott).
  • Not the old Word format (.doc, before 2007). Open it in Word or LibreOffice and save it as .docx first. Most e-mail and cloud services can also convert it for you.

Step by step

  1. Open the editor, then drop the document on the page or click Choose a file. To write from scratch, click Start a new document.
  2. The document opens on pages, as it will print. Edit it as in any word processor: type, select, change the style of a paragraph, add a table or a picture.
  3. Save. In Chrome and Edge, Save writes the changes back into the file you opened. In Firefox and Safari it downloads the edited copy.
  4. To send it in another format, use Download copy and choose Word, LibreOffice or PDF.

Your document stays on your device

The word processor is EdenText, an open-source program that runs entirely in your browser tab. The document is read from your disk and written back to it; it is not sent to us or to anyone else. While you work, an unsaved copy is kept in your browser. If the tab closes by accident, the start screen offers to resume it.

Send a PDF or a Word file?

  • Send a PDF when the document is finished: a CV, a letter, an invoice. It looks the same on every screen and printer, and nobody changes it by accident.
  • Send a Word or LibreOffice file when the other person must edit it: a draft to review, a form to complete, a template.

Received a PDF that you need to rework as a document? In the editor, Tools › Convert › Edit as a document opens its text as a Word document in a new tab. See how to convert a PDF to Word.

Fonts and layout

A document looks exactly the same only where its fonts are available. Word documents often use Calibri, Cambria or Aptos, which come with Microsoft Office and are not free to share. When a font is missing, a similar one is used instead: letters look slightly different, and a line or a page may break in another place.

Any word processor other than the one that made the file can show very complex layouts a little differently, such as text boxes anchored to pictures or fields that update themselves. Before sending an important document, look through it once, or download it as PDF and check the PDF.

Good habits

  • Keep the original: in Firefox and Safari the edited copy is a new download; in Chrome and Edge, use Download copy if you want to keep the file you opened unchanged.
  • Give versions clear names, such as report v2 or report – reviewed, rather than report final final.
  • Before sending a document outside your organisation, check its comments and its file properties (author, company). They travel with the file.
