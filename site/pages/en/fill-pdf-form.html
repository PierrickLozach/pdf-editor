How to fill in a PDF form, even one that is not fillable

A registration form from a school, a claim form from an insurer, a membership form from a club: many are sent as PDFs meant to be printed, filled in by hand and scanned. You can fill them in on screen instead, and the result is neater and easier to read.

Two kinds of PDF forms

  • Fillable forms have real fields: you click a box and a cursor appears. They are made with tools such as Adobe Acrobat.
  • Flat forms only have lines and boxes drawn on the page, like a sheet of paper. Most forms are flat: often a Word document saved as PDF.

To tell which one you have, open the PDF in your browser (drag it onto a Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari window) and click a box. If you can type, it is fillable.

A fillable form

Fill it in where you opened it: the PDF viewers built into browsers, Apple Preview and Adobe Acrobat Reader all fill form fields and save them. Then, if you need to sign it or add a picture, open the filled-in PDF in the editor and see how to sign a PDF.

A flat form, step by step

  1. Open the editor and open the form. It is not uploaded.
  2. Choose the Add text tool (shortcut T) and click just above the first line to fill in.
  3. Type your answer. In the bar, set the size (10 or 11 points matches most forms), the colour (dark blue or black) and bold if needed. Press Enter to place it.
  4. Move to the next field and repeat. To adjust a piece of text you added, choose Edit text and drag it, or click it to change what it says.
  5. To tick a box, add the text X in it.
  6. Add your signature with Tools › Sign.
  7. Save, then open the saved file once to check every page.

Tips for a neat result

  • Zoom in to place text precisely on the line, then zoom out to check the page as a whole.
  • Use one size and one colour for all your answers. Dark blue makes them easy to tell apart from the printed text.
  • Long answers: text is added one line at a time. For a box with several lines, add each line separately and keep the same left edge.
  • Dates: write them the way the form asks (DD/MM/YYYY or MM/DD/YYYY). Forms are read by people and sometimes by machines that expect one format.
  • Correcting yourself: undo with ⌘Z or Ctrl+Z, or click your text with Edit text and retype it.

Scanned forms

If the form was scanned or photographed, the page is a picture. You can still type on it in exactly the same way, because Add text writes on top of the picture. For a cleaner result, ask the sender for the original PDF if it exists.

Before you send it

  • Read every answer again, especially numbers: account numbers, dates of birth, amounts.
  • Forms often contain personal data. Send them through the channel the organisation asks for, and if you send by e-mail, consider protecting the PDF with a password given by phone.
  • If the organisation wants a handwritten signature on paper, an image of a signature may not be accepted: check their instructions.
