How to make a PDF from photos or scans

Receipts for an expense claim, a signed form photographed with your phone, the pages of a school report: one PDF is easier to send, print and file than a pile of pictures.

Step by step

  1. Open the editor. On the start screen, click Create a PDF from pictures.
  2. Select all the pictures at once (hold ⌘ on a Mac or Ctrl elsewhere to pick several, or Shift for a range).
  3. The PDF opens in the editor, one picture per page. Check the order in the page panel and drag pages to reorder them if needed.
  4. Save. The pictures are not uploaded: the PDF is made in your browser.

How the pages are made

  • Each picture is placed on an A4 page with a small margin, in portrait or landscape to match the picture, and scaled to fit without being cropped.
  • The pictures are sorted by file name, with numbers in their natural order: page 2 comes before page 10. Phone photos are named by the time they were taken, so they usually come out in the right order.
  • Photos taken with the phone held sideways are turned the right way up.

Taking photos that make a good PDF

The PDF can only be as readable as the photos. A few habits make a big difference:

  • Put the sheet flat on a dark, plain surface, so its edges stand out.
  • Use daylight or a bright room, and turn the flash off: it leaves a white glare in the middle of the page.
  • Hold the phone straight above the page, not at an angle, and fill the screen with the sheet.
  • Watch out for your own shadow, and check that the photo is sharp before you move to the next page.
  • Many phones have a document scanner built in: in the Notes app on an iPhone, or Google Drive on Android. It straightens and crops the page for you, and you can still assemble the results here.

Which pictures work

JPEG and PNG work in every browser. WebP, GIF and BMP work in recent browsers. HEIC, the format of iPhone photos, works in Safari. In Chrome, Edge or Firefox, set the iPhone camera to Settings › Camera › Formats › Most Compatible, or share the photos as JPEG. Other browsers show a message naming any picture they cannot read.

After making the PDF

  • Too large to send? Phone photos are big: ten of them can make a 30 MB PDF. See how to make a PDF smaller. Shrinking usually brings it down to a few megabytes with the text still sharp.
  • Need to search or copy the text? A photo has no text in it. Make it searchable with text recognition.
  • More pages to add? Save this PDF, then use + Add pages from a PDF… under the thumbnails to add another PDF, made from more pictures or not. See how to merge and reorder pages.
  • Copies of ID documents? Add a watermark saying who the copy is for before you send it.
