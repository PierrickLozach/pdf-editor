How to check that an online PDF tool does not upload your file

“Your files are safe with us” and “your files never leave your device” are two very different promises. Here is how to tell which one a tool keeps, in a few minutes and without being an expert.

Two kinds of online tools

Most online PDF tools work on their servers: your browser sends the document, the server processes it and sends the result back. The file then exists, at least for a while, on a computer you do not control. Many services delete files after an hour or two, and that may be acceptable for a menu or a flyer, but rarely for a payslip, a passport scan or a medical report.

Other tools, such as this editor, do the processing in your browser. The page downloads the program once, and then works on the document locally: there is nothing to delete afterwards, because the document was never sent.

Test 1: watch the network

  1. Open the tool's page in Chrome, Edge or Firefox.
  2. Open the developer tools: F12 on Windows, ⌥⌘I on a Mac. Choose the Network tab and clear the list.
  3. Open or drop a PDF, edit it and save it.
  4. Look at the requests that appear. A request whose size is close to your file's size, often of type POST or upload, means the document was sent. A few small requests (icons, fonts, statistics) are normal.

Test 2: go offline

  1. Open the tool's page and wait until it has fully loaded.
  2. Disconnect from the internet: turn off Wi-Fi, or unplug the network cable.
  3. Open a PDF and edit it. If it still works, the processing happens on your device. If the tool shows an error or keeps spinning, it needs its server.

This test is the easiest one to do, and the most convincing.

Test 3: read the policy and the code

  • Read the privacy policy: look for how long files are kept and where the servers are. Vague answers are a warning sign.
  • If the tool is open source, its code can be read by anyone, and claims can be verified. The code of this editor is on GitHub.

What this editor does

Your documents are opened, edited and saved in your browser, on your device. The site does count visits anonymously, in daily totals, and downloads free fonts by name when a document needs them, but it never receives your documents or their content. The privacy policy lists exactly what is sent.
