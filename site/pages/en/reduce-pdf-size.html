How to make a PDF smaller

An online form accepts 5 MB, your email client refuses attachments over 20 MB, and your PDF weighs 40. Here is why, and how to fix it.

What makes a PDF heavy

Text and drawings take very little space: a hundred pages of text often fit in less than 1 MB. What makes PDFs heavy is pictures: scanned pages, photos taken with a phone, or images inserted at full camera resolution. A single page scanned in colour at 600 dpi can weigh 5 to 10 MB on its own.

Shrinking a PDF therefore mostly means storing its pictures at a reasonable resolution, with good JPEG compression.

Step by step

  1. Open the editor and open the PDF.
  2. Click Tools, then Reduce file size, or use the button under the page thumbnails.
  3. Choose a level:
    • Smaller: about 200 dpi, still good for printing.
    • Smallest: about 120 dpi, for email and screens.
  4. The editor tells you the size before and after. If it is fine, save the PDF; otherwise undo with ⌘Z or Ctrl+Z.

Text stays sharp, searchable and editable: only pictures are recompressed. As an example, an 18 MB scanned document typically drops to about 1.3 MB at the Smaller level.

Other ways to save space

  • Remove pages you do not need, such as blank pages or appendices, with the delete button on each thumbnail.
  • Split the document into two PDFs if the limit is per file: Tools › Split or extract pages.
  • Scan at the right resolution: 150 to 200 dpi in colour is enough for most documents; 300 dpi in black and white for small print.

The editor opens PDFs of up to 250 MB. Beyond that, browsers run out of memory: reduce the file with another tool first, for example Preview on a Mac (File › Export, Quartz filter “Reduce File Size”).
