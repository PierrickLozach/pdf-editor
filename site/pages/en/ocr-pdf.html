How to make a scanned PDF searchable

You scanned a contract or a receipt, and now you cannot search it or copy a single word. Text recognition, called OCR, fixes that.

Why a scan has no text

A scanner or a phone camera produces a picture of the page. Saved as a PDF, it looks like a document, but for the computer it is still a photo: there are no letters to search, select or copy. OCR (optical character recognition) reads the picture, recognises the letters and adds an invisible text layer exactly on top of them.

Step by step

  1. Open the editor and open the scanned PDF.
  2. Click Tools, then Make searchable (OCR).
  3. Choose the language of the document, and a second language if the document mixes two.
  4. Start the recognition. It takes about 2 to 5 seconds per page, in your browser. The first time, the language data (1 to 3 MB) is downloaded once.
  5. Save the PDF. The pages look exactly the same, but you can now search them (⌘F or Ctrl+F in any PDF reader), select and copy their text.

The pages are recognised on your device. They are not sent to an online service, which matters for identity documents, payslips or medical records.

Getting good results

  • Resolution: 300 dpi is ideal for documents; 200 dpi is usually enough. Very small print benefits from 300 dpi in black and white.
  • Straight pages: scan the page flat and straight. Rotate sideways pages first with the rotate button on the page thumbnail.
  • Contrast: dark text on a light background. Shadows and creases from phone photos cause most errors.
  • The right language: it helps the recogniser with accents and special characters.

What OCR does not do

The recognised text is invisible and serves for searching and copying. It does not turn the scan into editable text: to change a word on a scanned page, white it out and type the new word with Add text. Handwriting is rarely recognised well.
